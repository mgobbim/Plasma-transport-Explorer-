RFX-mod · RFP magnetic topology

RFP profiles: cylindrical Taylor/Bessel reference. SHAx: synthetic m=1, n=7 nested helical surfaces. MH: optional synthetic multi-mode tearing perturbations in the actual guiding-centre field. Neither is an experimental RFX-mod equilibrium.

In tearing mode, δBᵣ/B sets a nominal RMS radial perturbation near r/a≈0.5. The guide field line and guiding-centre orbit are recomputed. A Poincaré section below tests the field-line topology. More perturbation can promote island overlap, but does not guarantee stochasticity. Try initial pitch ξ₀≈0.7 and Integration sample = Long to compare the two field choices; larger δBᵣ/B (e.g. 0.01) makes the difference stronger but is illustrative. SHAx is a separate nested-surface model.

Paper x axis: ν* · calculated from the selected plasma conditions using the PRL definition and the modeled local RFP q. A marker locates the interpolated SHAx or MH reference D.

δBᵣ/B sets the nominal tearing-mode amplitude in the MH orbit calculation, when that field is selected, and also sets the separate RR-ion benchmark. Lcorr enters only the RR benchmark. Neither changes the digitized ORBIT blue/black curves. The synthetic mode spectrum is not calibrated to the published discharges.

The reference D is obtained by log–log interpolation of published ORBIT data at the computed ν*; it is a paper lookup, not a calibrated local prediction.

Guiding-centre orbit example

Test-particle energy E₀ is independent of plasma Tₑ and Tᵢ used for transport. E₀ = ½mv² sets initial total kinetic energy, while pitch ξ₀ divides it into parallel and perpendicular motion. The energy slider is logarithmic (0.01–200 keV). Tokamak: ξ₀=v∥/v at the selected launch point (outboard midplane by default). Stellarator/RFP: ξ₀ at the selected 3D-field point (a sampled |B| minimum by default). Negative values reverse parallel motion.

Change any parameter to recalculate values and move the markers on the graph.

Interpretation: a regular invariant surface gives a ring or a discrete set of points in the section; magnetic islands can form chains and stochastic regions can scatter points across surfaces. A finite set of dispersed dots does not by itself prove global chaos. Both plots use identical launches and the same synthetic field model as the RFP orbit solver. They are shown side by side regardless of which MH field is selected for the orbit. The 12 tearing harmonics and their amplitude are illustrative, not an RFX-mod reconstruction. Near field reversal Bφ→0, φ is not a regular integration coordinate: integration stops if the line approaches that region. The RR transport benchmark remains independent.

Red: SHAx helical core · grey: nearly circular outer surfacesGold dot: single helical axis · separatrix expelled
SHAx topology: the magnetic island separatrix has been expelled: the former island O-point becomes the single helical magnetic axis, and there is no internal separatrix or X-point in this idealized SHAx core. The off-axis, elongated bean-shaped inner surfaces and nearly circular outer ones are constant-u surfaces of the same synthetic divergence-free magnetic field used for the guiding-centre orbit. Dots are successive field-line crossings, not particles. The angle-dependent shape is imposed smoothly (not obtained from SHEq or experimental data); a QSH/DAx state before separatrix expulsion would be a different topology, not plotted as a SHAx surface here. For comparison: Martines et al., Plasma Phys. Control. Fusion 53, 035015 (2011), Fig. 2; Martin et al., Nucl. Fusion 49, 104019 (2009), Fig. 6.
